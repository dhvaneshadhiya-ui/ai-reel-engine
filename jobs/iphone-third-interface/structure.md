# Structure — iphone-third-interface

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`styles/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).

## SHAPE (S17)

**Discovery.** Not a product announcement, and deliberately not a list.
The user's brand restriction forbids naming the app anywhere, which
rules out the announcement shape outright — an announcement needs a
subject to announce. Discovery is the shape that survives the
restriction and gains from it: the reel is "here is a way of working
you had not considered", the thing itself stays unnamed, and the
curiosity that creates is what the comment-gate CTA collects.

It also fixes the failure mode the brief itself warns about. Six
features in a row is a spec queue. Discovery forces a single question
under every feature — what does this let me stop doing? — and the
features then arrive in escalating order of how strange they are:
launch an app (obvious), run a Shortcut (useful), a website / a
timeline / an emoji (unexpected), throw a window away with a swipe
(the one nobody predicts).

## PROMISE (S2)

That the phone already sitting next to your Mac can do a job it is not
currently doing — be a third input surface — and that by the end you
will know concretely what that buys you.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: sentence one — "Your Mac has a third input device. It's in
your pocket." The loop is the word *third*: the viewer knows the first
two, and wants the third named.
Paid off: the payoff line returns to the same word — "the thing next
to your keyboard is a third interface" — after the features have
earned it. S18 satisfied: the ending closes on the opening's word, not
on a new idea.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: an iPhone app makes the phone a touch control surface for a Mac.
WHY: every Mac action worth repeating currently costs a hunt — the
Dock, Spotlight, Command-Tab — and all of that competes for the same
screen you are working on.
SO WHAT: the repeated actions move off the screen you are working on
and onto a surface that was already on the desk doing nothing. That is
the viewer-facing meaning, and it is why the payoff is about the desk,
not about the feature count.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Price ($2.99/mo, $29.99 lifetime, free tier). True and first-party,
  but a price on screen dates the reel and the CTA delivers the link
  anyway. Momentum beats density.
- visionOS support, version number 2.3, developer name, the 8-language
  list, the 8-pages limit, the PIN pairing flow. All real, none of it
  changes whether the viewer wants this.
- The "shot on iPhone 15 Pro, no AI" line. Genuinely interesting and a
  point in the developer's favour, but it is a fact about the vendor's
  marketing, not about what the viewer gets, and naming it invites the
  question "whose videos?" — which the brand restriction cannot answer.
- The grab-and-throw clipboard gesture. The gestures beat can only
  carry one idea in 2 seconds; minimize is the one that reads on mute.

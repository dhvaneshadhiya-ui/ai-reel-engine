# Structure — chatgpt-photo-prompts

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who assumes "AI photo editing" still means a face that drifts into someone else's
learns that ChatGPT's editor just got upgraded specifically to stop that — and that the
upgrade is exactly what makes a viral set of copy-paste prompts actually work, which
matters because the wording of the prompt, not the photo you start with, is what decides
whether you get a portrait or the plastic AI look everyone already recognizes.

## SHAPE (S17)

**List, with a spine** — same discipline as five-free-ai-tools. A bare "here are 9 prompts"
is a queue by prompt three. The spine here is the INSTRUCTION, not the aesthetic: every mode
shown is doing the same underlying thing (name a real camera/light/scene, then explicitly
protect identity and skin texture), so three examples teach the pattern instead of listing
nine names. The reel shows a representative few and hands over the rest via CTA rather than
reciting all nine on screen, which would flatten into a list a viewer tunes out of by mode 4.

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you know WHY these prompts work now (not six months ago), what the actual
instruction pattern is, and how to get the full set.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook's claim — "you don't need a photographer, you need the right sentence" —
promises that the SENTENCE, not the software, is the lever.
Paid off: the close names the one line every mode repeats (protect the face, protect the
skin texture) and ties it back to the hook: that is the sentence. The CTA then delivers the
full set, closing the loop with the actual artifact promised.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: OpenAI shipped Images 2.5 on Sept 8 — better identity preservation through edits,
more reliable multi-step instructions.
WHY: earlier models let your face drift toward a generic AI face the more you asked them to
change about the scene — precision editing without losing the subject is a genuinely hard,
previously-unsolved problem (independently confirmed, not just OpenAI's own framing).
SO WHAT: that reliability is what makes a long, specific prompt survive intact — which is
why a whole genre of "photographer mode" prompts started working and spreading this week.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Six of the nine named modes (Luxury Photo Shoot, travel-influencer look, Magazine Cover,
  Change Your Location, Professional Portrait). All real, all sourced, but reciting nine
  names is the list-not-a-story failure this format exists to avoid — three representative
  modes teach the pattern; the CTA delivers the rest instead of the reel reading them aloud.
- The API side of the announcement (GPT-Image-2.5 Flare/Sunburst, developer pricing). True
  and sourced, but it's a different audience (builders, not people editing selfies) and
  would need its own sentence to avoid implying the consumer app got a new price.
- Any claim that OpenAI built these specific prompts or endorsed this use. It did not — the
  reel is explicit that the prompts are a creator's own wording on top of the feature, not
  an OpenAI-shipped mode.
- Restaging or generating a "before/after" using one of the nine prompts ourselves. Doing so
  would put a photo of a real uploaded selfie's edit outcome in the reel, sourced to nobody,
  which is exactly the unsupported-visual failure the manifest's NOT CLAIMED section exists
  to block. The reel's visual proof of the capability is OpenAI's own official example
  pairs; the prompts are shown as text, sourced to their author.

## SOURCES

previously-unsolved problem); Rachel Woods' (@TechWith_Rachel) X thread, read in full with
every "Show more" expanded (single source, by nature — it's her own wording, quoted
directly, not a reported fact). Full list with VIA lines in research.md.

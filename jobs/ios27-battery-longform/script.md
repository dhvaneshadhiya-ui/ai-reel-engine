Your iPhone's battery has been dropping faster since you updated to iOS 27, and the internet has a lot of very confident answers about why. I spent this week going through what Apple has actually published about it, and almost none of those answers are in there. So in the next few minutes I'll show you what the phone is really doing to your battery after an update, the one screen that tells you whether it's that or something genuinely wrong, and the popular fix that Apple's own software chief says does nothing at all.

Start with what Apple says, because it's the one part of this story that isn't guesswork. On its support page about a battery draining too quickly, Apple writes that even though you can use your device immediately after an update, certain tasks related to the update keep running in the background, and that those tasks can affect battery life and thermal performance. So your phone isn't broken, it's busy, and if it has been running warm as well, that's the same thing showing up in a different way.

Apple does name the work, too. Your phone is indexing your files so that search can find them, downloading new assets, and updating your apps, and none of that is visible while you're using it. Which is exactly why your battery seems to be draining for no reason: the reason is real, it's just happening somewhere you can't see.

Except that on iOS 27, there is somewhere you can see it. If your phone is still working through that list, open Settings and look just above General, and you'll find a row that isn't normally there at all: Optimising Search and Siri. That isn't a setting you can change, it's a status, and it's your phone telling you it hasn't finished yet.

I recorded that in Apple's own iOS 27 simulator, and within the hour, once the indexing finished, the row was simply gone, which is the clearest evidence you'll get that this work ends.

That brings up the question everyone actually wants answered, which is how long it lasts. Apple's answer is one sentence, and there is no number anywhere in it. If you notice that your battery life has decreased after updating your device, it says, wait a few days and then check again. Not four days. Not seventy-two hours. A few days.

Everyone else has been far more specific. Three to five days. About four days. Twenty-four to forty-eight hours. I tried tracing those back, and they don't lead anywhere: no test, no measurement, no Apple page behind any of them. One of the few outlets that went looking said it plainly, that every confident number circulating that week came from somewhere other than Apple.

And if this feels familiar, it should, because it runs on a schedule. Same week last September, iOS 26 drained batteries and got that same sentence from Apple. The year before that, iOS 18. You are watching the third September in a row of it.

So here's the screen I promised you. Open Settings, then Battery, and look near the top for a note saying that a recent software update is finishing in the background. If that note is there, you have your answer: it's the update, it isn't your battery, and you can close the forum tabs.

What won't help is the thing most people try first, which is swiping every app out of the switcher. Back in 2017, a customer emailed Apple's software chief to ask whether he force quits apps, and whether doing it preserves battery life, and his reply, published by Daring Fireball, was two words: no and no. iOS freezes the apps you aren't using, so killing them just means the phone has to load them again from scratch, which costs more power rather than less.

Your battery health is fine as well, and you can settle that one with arithmetic. Maximum capacity is counted in full charge cycles: Apple rates an iPhone 14 or earlier to keep eighty percent of its capacity after five hundred of them, and an iPhone 15 or later after a thousand. One rough week cannot move a number defined across hundreds of cycles.

So check that one screen, leave the app switcher alone, and look again in three days. If the update note has gone and the battery is still draining, that's the point where it's worth taking further. And if you're in the middle of this, drop your iPhone model in the comments with how many days yours took to settle, because when I went looking for anyone who had measured this drain, in September 2026, I couldn't find a single benchmark or screen-on-time test from any outlet. That absence is half the problem.

# Structure — whatsapp-guest-calls

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who has given up on getting the one person without WhatsApp onto a
call discovers they can now send that person a link that needs no account and
no app — which matters because it removes the "download this first" wall, and
the one condition attached (a computer browser) decides exactly who it helps.

## SHAPE (S17)

**News, carried by a planted catch.** This is a real, official launch
(WhatsApp's own blog, 14 Oct 2026), so News is the honest certainty. A plain
announcement would be a feature list; the catch (guests join from a computer
browser) is the tension that turns it into a story with a payoff, so the
shape is News with one open loop running through the how-to.

## PROMISE (S2)

You will learn how to get anyone onto a WhatsApp call without them signing
up, what a guest can and cannot do once inside, and the one condition that
decides who this is really for.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: sentence 3 — "But the catch sits on the guest's side, and it
decides who this really helps. Hold on to that."
Paid off: "About that catch. WhatsApp's own steps say the guest opens the link
on a computer. Not on their phone." The next line returns to the
loop's own words ("who this really helps") by naming who it is for: the client
on a work laptop, the relative at a desk (S18).

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: a WhatsApp user creates a call link; anyone can join it from a browser
with no account, free, no time limit, up to 32 people.
WHY: the host stays in control — a waiting room, approval, removal — and the
guest is fenced: no phone numbers, no messages, no adding people.
SO WHAT: the person who would never install WhatsApp can now be on the call,
as long as they are at a computer. It is a desk-and-laptop feature at launch,
not a phone one.

## CONFIRMATION BEAT (2-5s)

Right after the hook: WhatsApp's own blog headline, captured on MOBILE, on
screen while "WhatsApp just launched it. It's called guest calling. No
account, no cost, no time limit." is spoken — the headline literally reads
"no account needed, no cost, no time limit". The viewer sees the official
source saying the exact words they just heard.

## VIEWER QUESTIONS

- Q: Do they have to download WhatsApp or sign up?  A: "No account, no cost, no time limit."
- Q: Can a random person with the link just walk in?  A: "Nobody gets in until you let them."
- Q: Will a stranger see my phone number?  A: "they can't see anyone's phone number"
- Q: Is it still private / encrypted?  A: "the call itself stays end-to-end encrypted"
- Q: Can they join on their phone?  A: "the guest opens the link on a computer. Not on their phone."
- Q: How do I start one?  A: "open the Calls tab, tap the plus, and create a call link."

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- **Call links themselves are not new** (Zuckerberg announced them in Sept
  2022, also 32 people). True and a nice "it only took four years" angle, but
  it spends a beat on history while the viewer wants the how-to.
- **Guest chats (text) already exist**, per WABetaInfo. Single-sourced and a
  different feature; it would blur what launched.
- **Comparison to Zoom's free 40-minute limit.** It is what gives "no time
  limit" its weight, but the only sources found were community posts and a
  2020 university notice, not Zoom's current pricing page. Not spoken.
- **FaceTime comparison** (Android Headlines). An outlet's framing, not a fact
  that changes the viewer's understanding.
- **Raise hand / thumbs-up and heart detail** — kept as "raise a hand, or
  react", the emoji names cut for momentum.
- **The calendar plugin install steps.** Kept as one forward-looking line; the
  steps are not available yet.
- **Regional availability** — not stated by WhatsApp; coverage says it is
  rolling out. Covered in the caption, not the voice.

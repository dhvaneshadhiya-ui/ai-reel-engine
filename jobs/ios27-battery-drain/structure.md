# Structure — ios27-battery-drain

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

Someone who updated to iOS 27 watches the battery fall faster than it did last
week and assumes the phone, or the battery, is damaged. The real explanation is
written in Apple's own support pages — background work that Apple refuses to put
a clock on — and the numbers everyone is repeating came from somewhere else
entirely. Which matters because the two things people do in response (panic about
battery health, swipe away every card in the switcher) are the two things that
cannot help.

## SHAPE (S17)

Myth-busting. Not news: there is no event, no Apple statement, no confirmed bug
and nothing measured by anybody. What there IS, is a story the internet is
telling with numbers nobody can trace, against an Apple page that gives no
number at all. Myth-busting is also the shape that earns the reassurance rather
than asserting it: each belief gets a receipt, ending with the one measurement
that settles whether the battery itself is fine.

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you know what your iPhone is actually doing, which single screen tells
you whether it is that or something else, and which popular fix is worthless.

## OPEN LOOP (S2, S10)

The hook says the phone is busy, not broken, but not with what — the answer lands
at the end as a thing to check, and the loop closes on the Battery screen note.
Second loop: "what that page never gives you is a deadline" promises a specific
omission and pays it off in the next breath.

## CONFIRMATION BEAT (2-5s)

Apple's own support page, captured on mobile and filmed, decelerating onto the
sentence: "certain tasks related to the update continue in the background and
might affect battery life and thermal performance." The viewer sees the claim is
Apple's, in Apple's words, three seconds in — before any of our graphics.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT

WHAT: the battery falls faster after the update.
WHY: Apple documents post-update background work — indexing for search,
downloading assets, updating apps — and gives no duration for it.
SO WHAT: check the Battery screen for the update note instead of panicking; the
force-quit habit does nothing, Maximum Capacity cannot move in a week, and the
honest test is to look again in three days — and since nobody has measured this,
the ask is for the viewer's own reading, not a keyword.

## VIEWER QUESTIONS

- Q: Is my battery damaged, or is my phone broken? A: "maximum capacity is measured across hundreds of charge cycles, and one rough week can't move it."
- Q: What is the phone actually doing? A: "Your phone is indexing your stuff for search, downloading new assets, and updating apps."
- Q: How long does this last? A: "Not four days, not seventy-two hours. Apple's actual words are, wait a few days, then check again."
- Q: How do I tell whether it is the update or a real problem? A: "Open Settings, then Battery. If a note says a recent update is still finishing in the background, that's your answer."
- Q: Should I close all my apps to save battery? A: "What won't help is swiping away all your apps."
- Q: Is there an update that fixes it? A: NOT ANSWERED — no iOS 27.0.1 exists as of 2026-09-19, so the reel gives no fix timeline instead of predicting one.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- The Siri AI re-indexing explanation — the reference article's own inference, with
  no source, contradicted in spirit by an independent audit.
- Every duration number in circulation, and every "millions affected" framing.
- The 83%-to-37% Reddit figure: one person, quoted everywhere as if representative.
- 5G: a real power cost, but not this cause, and calling it a myth would be wrong.
- Any iPhone Settings screenshot: Apple's only public Battery image is iOS 26, and we
  have no iOS 27 device to capture. The receipts are the support pages themselves.

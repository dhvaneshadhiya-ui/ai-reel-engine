# Structure — 10-ai-tools-free-vs-paid

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who assumes "free" means the same thing on every AI tool discovers
that some free tiers are real, renewing plans while others are a one-time
credit grant dressed up as free, which matters because it decides whether
the tool they build a habit around is still there next week or already
spent.

## SHAPE (S17)

**List, with a spine** — the same pattern as `five-free-ai-tools`. A bare
list of ten prices is forgettable by item four; the spine here is FREE VS
TRIAL, not just FREE VS PAID: every item answers not only "what does paid
buy" but "is this free tier a plan or a bucket you empty once." That
distinction is real (confirmed independently for 3 of the 10: Runway,
Gamma, and Notion AI's AI layer) and it is what turns ten unrelated price
facts into one idea.

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you know, for ten real tools across different jobs, exactly what
free gives you, whether that free tier renews or runs out, and exactly what
the paid price buys on top of it.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook — "They don't all mean it the same way" — promises that
the word "free" itself is doing different jobs across the ten, not that
this is a flat list of ten prices.
Paid off: the close — "So the next time something says free, remember: they
don't all mean it the same way. Ask whether it's a plan, or a trial wearing
a better word" — repeats the hook's own words, then turns them into a
question the viewer can carry past this video, to any tool (S18).

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: ten AI tools, each with a free tier and a named paid price that
unlocks something specific.
WHY: as usage-based compute costs bite, some vendors default to a
renewing-but-capped allowance (a real plan) while others default to a
one-time credit grant that reads as "free" on the pricing page but behaves
like a trial once it's spent — the same word, two different product
decisions.
SO WHAT: before a viewer builds a habit around a "free" AI tool, one
question — does this refill, or did I already spend it — tells them whether
it's still free next month.

## CONFIRMATION BEAT (2-5s)

Right after the hook, a `logos` slide block shows all ten tool marks
together (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Canva, Notion, Runway, Descript,
Cursor, Leonardo.Ai, Gamma) before a single price is spoken — proving "ten
real, named tools" the same way five-free-ai-tools' hook slide did.

## VIEWER QUESTIONS

- Q: Is "free" the same thing on every one of these tools? A: "They don't all mean it the same way."
- Q: What's the actual catch on the ones that aren't a real ongoing plan? A: "Runway's free credits are a hundred twenty-five, once, about five seconds of video, then gone."
- Q: Does the paid price buy something specific, or just "more of the same"? A: "Pro, twenty dollars, unlocks both, adds Claude Code, and multiplies your usage by five."
- Q: Will these numbers still be right next month? A: "though Cursor's reportedly changed billing twice this year."

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- A ranking or "best value" verdict — no comparative test was run; stating
  one would be exactly the kind of unsupported claim G14/G15 exist to
  catch. The reel gives the viewer the facts to decide for their own job
  instead.
- Notion AI's free-response count as a hard number — Notion's own page says
  only "a limited number"; the ~20 figure floating on review sites is
  single-source and unconfirmed, so it's cut rather than spoken as fact.
- ChatGPT's Go ($8) and Pro ($200) tiers — a third and fourth stop would
  blur the one free-vs-paid pair this reel makes per tool; each tool gets
  exactly one comparison, not a full pricing ladder.
- Cursor's specific credit-dollar equivalents and Auto-billing mechanics —
  third-party trackers disagree with each other and none was confirmed on
  cursor.com directly; the script states only the four official headline
  prices plus one honest hedge that billing has changed.
- Leonardo.Ai's in-progress move to pay-as-you-go pricing — unconfirmed as
  a stable structure while it's actively rolling out; the script uses the
  subscription tiers live on the page today, not a structure still in
  flux.
- Gamma's "per-edit credit charges" complaint — single-source (a Trustpilot
  rollup), not independently confirmed; not spoken.

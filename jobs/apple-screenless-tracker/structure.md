# Structure — apple-screenless-tracker

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`frameworks/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who just watched Apple brag about the Watch's new all-day heart
sensor (Sept 9) discovers, thirteen days later, that Bloomberg says Apple is
already building a completely different device to compete with Whoop and
Oura — and it deliberately has no screen at all — which matters because it
means Apple thinks the Watch's real weakness was never the sensor, it was
the screen.

## SHAPE (S17)

**News**, with a contrarian-reveal spine: the obvious read ("Apple is
catching up on hardware") is wrong, and the script exists to correct it
mid-way through. This fits the material because the timing itself (Sept 9
official launch -> Sept 22 leak) is the story's real hook, not just "Apple
is building a gadget."

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end, the viewer understands why Apple would build a screen-free
tracker two weeks after finally giving the Watch all-day heart tracking —
and why that means Apple no longer believes a smartwatch is the only shape
health tracking has to take.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook's implicit question — "Apple just fixed the sensor gap,
so why is it also building a device with no screen at all?"
Paid off: the MECHANISM beat ("this was never about the hardware... it's
about whether people want a screen on their wrist at all") — the CTA then
hands that exact question back to the viewer.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: Bloomberg reports Apple is prototyping a screen-free fitness band.
WHY: Apple's own health chief, Eddy Cue, has been pushing for interfaces
simpler than the Watch's since taking over health last October — and the
Watch's September upgrade proves the sensor was never the limiting factor.
SO WHAT: the real fight isn't "better sensors," it's whether a screen
belongs on your wrist at all for fitness tracking — and Apple, Whoop ($10B)
and Oura ($15.6B IPO) are all now betting on the same answer.

## CONFIRMATION BEAT (2-5s)

Right after the hook: a mobile screenshot receipt of the MacRumors/9to5Mac
Sept 22 2026 headline reporting the Bloomberg story, proving the report
itself is real (not speculation invented for the reel) before any claim
about the device is elaborated.

## VIEWER QUESTIONS

- Q: Wait, doesn't the Apple Watch already track heart rate all the time now? A: "that sensor gap was already closed in September" (then the pivot to why that's not the answer)
- Q: Who says this is real, is this confirmed by Apple? A: "Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports Apple is testing a completely different device" (explicitly not Apple-confirmed, hedged throughout)
- Q: What would this actually look and feel like? A: "It's reportedly a thin fabric band with a sensor module on your wrist. No display. No notifications."
- Q: Is this actually going to happen, and when? A: "It's not landing before 2028, if it lands at all." (explicitly hedged, not a promise)
- Q: Why would Apple bother if the Watch is already winning? A: "Oura just filed to go public at a sixteen billion dollar valuation." (real money says screen-free isn't a niche)

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Google's Fitbit Air (May 2026) and Garmin's screenless device (PYMNTS
  context) — cut to keep focus on Apple vs. Whoop/Oura, the framing the
  Bloomberg report itself uses; naming three more competitors would dilute
  the single comparison the hook sets up.
- Apple's Sonera acquisition (neural-signal sensors) — interesting but not
  tied by any source specifically to this project; would imply a connection
  the reporting doesn't make.
- The MacRumors forum split reaction (enthusiastic vs. skeptical of Gurman)
  — good color, but adding a "here's what people think" beat would cost a
  clean CTA moment; the CTA question already invites that reaction directly.
- Mark Gurman's specific accuracy percentage (86.5%, AppleTrack) — the
  number's own sourcing is soft (a fan-tracking site, not methodologically
  rigorous), so it's left out rather than stated as if it were solid; the
  script stays hedged throughout instead of leaning on an accuracy claim to
  justify confidence.
- Apple's rumored prior smart-ring project — single, thin sourcing
  (thetechportal.com only); not load-bearing enough to use as the honesty
  beat, which instead rests on Bloomberg's own "hasn't decided" / "technology
  investigation" / "2028" language.

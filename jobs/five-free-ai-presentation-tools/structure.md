# Structure — five-free-ai-presentation-tools

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who believes free AI presentation tools are either useless trials or one app that
does everything discovers that five genuinely free tools each take a DIFFERENT slow part of a
deck off their hands (the draft, the file, the edits, the layout, the diagram), which matters
because each free plan stops somewhere different, and knowing where lets you stack them
instead of burning one tool's credits on everything.

## SHAPE (S17)

**List**, with a spine. Two spines run through every item, always in the same order: the JOB
it does (one verb: turns a prompt into a deck, turns notes into a file, fixes by chat, turns a
list into a timeline, turns a paragraph into a diagram) and then WHERE FREE STOPS. The
repetition is the rhythm of a list reel; the different job per tool is what stops it being five
versions of "AI makes slides". The close cashes the jobs in as a workflow.

## PROMISE (S2)

Five free tools, the part of the deck each one does, and exactly where each free plan stops.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: "Each free plan just stops somewhere different." — the viewer now watches every item
for its ceiling.
Paid off: "So stack them: draft in Claude, import the file into Gamma, and spend credits only
on the slides that matter." The ending returns to the ceilings one level up (S18): the limits
are the reason to combine the tools, not a reason to skip them.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: five tools with free plans and published ceilings.
WHY: each handles a different slow part of a deck — and each rations it differently (one-time
credits, weekly credits, usage limits, branding, a missing export).
SO WHAT: use the unlimited-feeling one (Designer, already in PowerPoint on the web) for layout,
the file-maker (Claude) for the draft, and save the one-time credits (Gamma, Pitch) for the
slides where design matters.

## CONFIRMATION BEAT (2-5s)

Right after the hook, Gamma's own help-centre demo: the Generate screen with a real prompt
typed ("how to make a matcha") and "Generate outline" pressed, then the outline filling in —
the product visibly turning one prompt into a deck, on a `screen` block with a focus move.

## VIEWER QUESTIONS

- Q: Is it actually free or just a trial?  A: "four hundred credits at signup that never top up"
- Q: Can I get a real PowerPoint file out of it?  A: "Free Claude makes them into a real PowerPoint file"
- Q: Will it put a watermark on my deck?  A: "a Gamma badge on exports"
- Q: I already use PowerPoint. Is anything in there free?  A: "On the web, Designer turns a bullet list into a timeline, free for everyone"
- Q: Which one should I use?  A: "draft in Claude, import the file into Gamma"

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Canva: the obvious name, but whether Free can run its presentation generator is contradicted
  by a June 2026 hands-on and Canva's own eligibility table. Cut rather than hedged.
- Google Slides + Gemini and Copilot in PowerPoint: both paid for ordinary accounts.
- Presentations.AI, Kimi, Genspark, SlidesGPT, Plus AI, Beautiful.ai: trial, card, paid export,
  or an unpublished ceiling.
- Paid prices. Only Pitch and Claude have them cleanly on the vendor page, and five prices would
  double the numbers per item without helping the viewer choose. The catch is what they need.
- Napkin Slides (beta decks): its free PPT export contradicts Napkin's pricing page.
- Credits-per-deck conversions: no vendor publishes one.

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who assumes "security camera" means "records everything" discovers
Apple's version reportedly does the opposite — it watches, decides, and
tells you, but by design tries not to keep the footage — which matters
because Apple is about to ask people to pay a subscription for that, under a
brand-new CEO, on a product category Apple has already walked away from
once.

## SHAPE (S17)

News (single-report explainer, format=news).

WHY: this is one report (Gurman/Bloomberg), not a multi-item roundup, and
its news value isn't just "Apple is making a camera" — it's the inversion
(a camera built not to record) plus the context (first big bet under a new
CEO, on a category Apple has a shaky record in). That's a News/Discovery
hybrid, told straight, not a list.

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you'll know exactly what Apple's camera reportedly does
differently, what it plugs into, when it's coming — and why none of that is
locked in yet.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook states the camera "isn't built to record you" without yet
saying what it IS built to do — that gap is the loop.
Paid off: mid-script, when the AI-watches/metadata-only mechanism is
explained, and the loop closes literally at the moment the "so what" lands
(a camera that mostly doesn't watch is a strange thing to sell as
"security" — which is the tension the ending returns to).

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: Apple is reportedly building a camera that uses on-device AI to watch
a room and mostly sends metadata, not continuous video, plus a paid
monitoring service to go with it, for 2027.
WHY: it's a privacy-forward architecture — less raw footage stored or
uploaded means less to leak, subpoena, or misuse — and a second, durable
subscription business layered on top of hardware Apple already sells.
SO WHAT: it's also a completely different definition of "security camera"
than Ring, Nest or Apple's own HomeKit Secure Video today use, arriving
under Apple's first new CEO in 15 years, on a timeline that has already
slipped at least twice, from a company that quietly abandoned the last
piece of home-security hardware it tried (HomeKit Secure Routers). None of
that means it won't happen — it means "reportedly" is doing real work here.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- The separate home hub product (expected end of 2026) and a rumored video
  doorbell — real, but different products on a different timeline; naming
  both would blur which claim belongs to which device in 60-80s.
- Exact iCloud+ storage-tier pricing for today's HomeKit Secure Video — true
  and sourced, but a number-heavy tangent that doesn't change the throughline;
  kept as one unquantified line ("Apple already sells storage for this") to
  set up the subscription framing instead.
- Ring/Nest competitive positioning — accurate context, but adding a second
  brand comparison on top of the Apple-vs-Apple-past framing (routers) would
  split the reel's one point in two.
- The MacRumors forum reaction itself — real audience skepticism, but it's
  a comment thread, not evidence; it shaped the ending's framing ("is that
  still security?") without being cited as if it were a source.

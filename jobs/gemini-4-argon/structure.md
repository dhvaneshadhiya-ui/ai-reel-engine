# Structure — gemini-4-argon

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`frameworks/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A Gemini user who hears "Google's most powerful model yet" discovers it spent
its first two weeks with cyber defenders only because it can find and patch
security holes by itself, which matters because that same skill set, plus its
limits and price, decides whether it is worth paying for now.

## SHAPE (S17)

News, built on one withheld reason. The hook states an odd fact (defenders
got it first) and withholds why; the why is the headline capability, so the
capability list hangs off it instead of reading as a spec sheet. Then the
honest check (ties Astra; Bloomberg's coding report) and the price clock.

## PROMISE (S2)

What Google's new top model can do, who can use it today, and why Google
handed it to cyber defenders before anyone else.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: "and Google had a reason for that order."
Paid off: "The reason is security. Google says Argon can find security holes,
test them and patch them by itself." The ending returns to it: "a model that
can patch code by itself" in the comment question (S18).

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: Gemini 4 Argon, Google's flagship, live Oct 13 for AI Pro and Ultra.
WHY: it can find and patch vulnerabilities, so Google gave defenders the
first look and ships the public version with attack refusals.
SO WHAT: you get a 1M-token, text-out model that ties GPT-6 Astra on an
independent index but may be weaker at real coding; developers pay $2 per
million input tokens until Nov 30, then double.

## CONFIRMATION BEAT (2-5s)

Right after the hook: Google's own key art "Gemini 4 Argon" (launch post
hero), then the Oct 13 Gemini app post captured on mobile, its line "Google AI
Pro and Ultra subscribers can now access Gemini 4 Argon" highlighted.

## VIEWER QUESTIONS

- Q: Can I use it?  A: "it's live in the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search for Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers"
- Q: What can it actually do?  A: "Google says Argon can find security holes, test them and patch them by itself"
- Q: Is it better than ChatGPT?  A: "the independent Artificial Analysis index has it tied with OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra"
- Q: Can it make images or talk?  A: "it only answers in text, so it won't make images or audio"
- Q: What does it cost developers?  A: "it's two dollars per million input tokens until November thirtieth. After that, the price doubles"

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Google's own benchmark wins (DeepSWE 77.9%, LVBench 91.7%, etc.): vendor numbers; the independent tie is the honest headline.
- Quantum, Rust migration, Chromium refactor demos: impressive but each needs explaining.
- Long decode (1M output): needs a caveat (default 65K).
- Antigravity, AI Studio, Android Studio, Ultra-only developer surfaces: developer detail.
- Subscription prices: no Google page showed them.
- $400M scrapped 3.5 Pro: Bloomberg Intelligence estimate via a secondary.
- Unverified hallucination / ProgramBench numbers: snippet-only sources.

You just can't tell a photo from an AI fake anymore. But here's what's actually new: the proof now starts before your phone even finishes taking the picture.

It's called Apple Reference Image, live on the iPhone 18 Pro.

The sensor signs the pixels the instant light hits it, before anything else can touch them. That signed file goes to Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which checks it and signs the final photo with encryption strong enough to survive quantum computers. It doesn't carry your name or your location, only proof that an Apple sensor captured those exact pixels, one Apple can revoke if that sensor is ever compromised.

Here's why that matters. Leica, Sony, Nikon, and Google's Pixel use the existing standard. It signs a photo after it's already been processed. Apple signs it before any software touches it at all, so there's no window for a fake to get swapped in first.

Anyways, it comes switched off, built for photojournalists and courtrooms, not your everyday shot. And it's not perfect. SquaredTech points out it can't tell you the photo wasn't staged, only that the pixels weren't.

The proof still starts at the sensor, not in an app after the fact.

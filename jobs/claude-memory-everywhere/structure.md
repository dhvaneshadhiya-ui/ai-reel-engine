# Structure — claude-memory-everywhere

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`frameworks/short-form-master.md` §4 (story engine), `styles/shortform-script-framework.md`
(S17 shapes; S25 standard).

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who assumes an AI assistant's memory is a black box — something it
quietly accumulates about you and you just live with — discovers that Claude's
memory is now a list of short text files they can open, correct, or delete, and
that the same memory follows them from chat into Cowork; which matters because a
wrong fact about you can now be fixed ONCE instead of re-argued in every future
conversation.

## SHAPE (S17)

**Product announcement**, built as misconception -> reality -> proof -> limit ->
consequence. Not the four-section feature list the announcement itself is
organised as.

**REBALANCED 2026-08-26 after a user challenge: does the script actually deliver
the headline?** Measured, and it did not. The first draft spent 85% of its words
on "you decide what's in it" and 10% on "memory works everywhere" — one sentence,
two of twenty-five beats. The word "everywhere" never appeared; neither did web,
desktop or mobile; "Cowork" appeared once behind a four-word gloss; and
Anthropic's own stated benefit (less re-explaining) was absent. Both independent
outlets led on the half the draft buried.

The fix was structural, not cosmetic. The two halves are not separate facts —
**the hinge that joins them is Anthropic's own line, "a fix pays off
everywhere."** Correcting one file only matters BECAUSE the memory is
everywhere. So: reach comes first (it is the news), control lands on top of it,
and one sentence carries both — "fix your company's old name in that file, and
every conversation after gets it right — in chat, and in Cowork." Reach is now
27% of the script and seeded in the hook.

## PROMISE (S2)

You will see exactly what Claude stores about you, what it will not store even
if you ask, and where the switch is.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: sentence 3 — "But here's the part that matters most: some of it Claude
refuses to write down."
Paid off: sentence 10-12 — "Back to what it refuses to write down. Social
Security numbers, government IDs, criminal history, immigration status. Those
never get saved either way..."
Second, quieter loop: the hook's "Claude keeps a file on you" returns as the
closing line, "The file on you didn't go away. You just got the key." (S18)

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

- WHAT: memory is unified across chat and Cowork, and exposed as an editable
  list of topic files; sensitive categories are opt-in; some categories are
  refused outright.
- WHY: memory stored as short, named, human-readable files is auditable. The
  thing you can see is the thing you can fix.
- SO WHAT: one correction propagates to every later conversation, and the
  categories Claude refuses are a floor you cannot lower by accident.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- **"Import memory from other AI providers"** — visible in Anthropic's own
  screenshot, but it is a MARCH 2026 feature, not part of this announcement.
  Including it would have implied it was new. (MacRumors 2026-03-02.)
- The three worked examples in the blog (manager update, conference agenda, QBR
  deck) — three illustrations of one point; kept none, since the Cowork beat
  needs one sentence, not three.
- **"Memory updates as you chat"** — one of the announcement's four named
  sections, and it was in the draft ("say your deadline moved to September, and
  the next chat already knows"). Cut at the rehearsal stage: it was the only
  beat that did not serve the spine (see it -> fix it -> it follows you -> what
  it refuses), and it was the 15 words between a 79-85s prediction and the
  60-80s band. Recorded because it is the one real fact this reel omits.
- Pause / reset memory, and the "files are short" clause — momentum over
  completeness.
- The Team/Enterprise detail is kept to one clause because a business viewer
  needs to know their admin decides, and nothing more.

## NUMBERS — a deliberate exception, recorded

`check_script.py` advises one number every 2-3 sentences; this script has one
every 16. That is not an oversight. The substance of this announcement is
CATEGORIES and CONTROLS, not specs — there is no benchmark, price or dimension
to report. Manufacturing numbers ("five sensitive categories", "four refused
types") would be padding a count, not reporting a fact. Accepted, with reason.

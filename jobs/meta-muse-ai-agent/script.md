Meta just called its new AI agent the world's first personal AI agent. But the catch is bigger than that.

It's called Muse, and Zuckerberg says it'll work 24/7 so you don't have to.

Meaning it doesn't just chat back. It emails people, books your flights, and haggles your bill down. It'll even turn a recipe you saved off Instagram into a grocery list.

All of that needs real access to your accounts. So Muse runs inside its own secure computer, walled off from the rest of the internet. And a separate watchdog called Sentinel has to approve anything before it leaves that computer. Meta says Muse never even sees your password or your card.

The catch: Muse isn't the first personal AI agent. Four other companies already shipped agents like this before Meta did: Google, OpenAI, Amazon and Anthropic.

Not to mention, the FTC once fined this same company five billion dollars over how it handled people's data.

For now, Muse launches in the US only, on the phone or the web, free to start. Want more? That's $20 or $100 a month.

So before you let Muse work 24/7 on your behalf, is Meta really who you want holding the keys?

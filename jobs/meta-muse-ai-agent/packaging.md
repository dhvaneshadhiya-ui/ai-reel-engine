  Meta just launched Muse and called it, in its own headline, the world's
  first personal AI agent.

  What it actually does

  • Emails people back, books your flights, haggles your bill down
  • Can turn a recipe you saved off Instagram into a grocery list
  • Runs inside its own secure computer (the "Muse Secure VM"), with a
    separate watchdog called Sentinel that has to approve anything before
    it reaches the internet
  • Meta says Muse never sees your password or your card

  The catch the headline skips

  It isn't the first personal AI agent. Google (Project Mariner), OpenAI
  (Operator / ChatGPT Work), Amazon (Nova Act) and Anthropic (Computer
  Use) all shipped agents that browse, book and buy on your behalf before
  this one launched.

  Worth knowing before you connect your accounts

  This is the same company the FTC fined five billion dollars in 2019 over
  how it handled people's data.

  Availability

  Live now in the US only, on the phone or the web. Free to start, with
  $20 and $100 monthly tiers for people who want more.

  So — before you let something work on your behalf 24/7, is Meta really
  who you want holding the keys?

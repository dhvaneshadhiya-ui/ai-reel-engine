# Structure — meta-muse-ai-agent

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`styles/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).
`script_approval.py propose` refuses while placeholder angle-bracket fields remain.

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who takes "world's first personal AI agent" at face value
discovers it's Meta's own marketing framing, not a fact — Google, OpenAI,
Amazon and Anthropic all shipped agents like this first — which matters
because the real question isn't who's first, it's whether you hand a
company with a $5B privacy fine on its record your inbox, calendar and
bank account.

## SHAPE (S17)

**News, with a myth-busting spine on the "first" claim.**
What happened (Meta launched Muse, calling it the world's first) → why
that claim doesn't hold (the competitive field already existed) → what it
actually does and how Meta says it's locked down → what happens next
(you decide whether to grant it access). Chosen because the interesting
story here isn't the launch itself — it's the gap between the superlative
in Meta's own headline and the crowded field it's launching into, plus the
trust question that follows once you look past the headline.

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you'll know exactly what Muse can actually do, whether "world's
first" is true, and what you're really weighing before you let it touch
your email and your bank account.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook itself — "Meta just called its new AI agent the world's
first personal AI agent." (a claim stated, not yet checked)
Paid off: the myth-bust line ("Except it isn't...") lands mid-script once
the viewer has seen what Muse does and why the claim sounded plausible;
the ending returns to the real stakes the "first" framing was distracting
from — trusting Meta specifically, not novelty.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: Muse is a chat-based agent that acts across your apps — emails,
books travel, negotiates bills, fills out forms, plans your dinner party.
WHY: Meta's pitch is a locked architecture (Secure VM + Sentinel) built
specifically because that access is dangerous if it goes wrong.
SO WHAT: The "first" claim is marketing, not a fact — so the actual
decision a viewer faces is not "is this new," it's "do I trust the
company with the FTC's $5B fine on its file enough to hand it my inbox
and my card."

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Full pricing breakdown (what exactly $20 vs $100 unlocks) — one number
  (US-only, free + $20/$100) carries the point; a tier-by-tier feature
  list is density without payoff at this length.
- Muse Spark backstory and the April 2026 model launch — different news
  cycle, would force explaining Meta's model family mid-reel for no payoff.
- "Muse Image" opt-out consent backlash (July 2026) — a different product
  under a shared brand name; including it would misattribute a real but
  separate controversy to this launch (see research.md NOT USED).
- The single-sourced Forkast report on internal guardrail bypasses — not
  corroborated anywhere else; would need a heavy hedge that costs more
  words than the claim is worth at 70s (see research.md NOT USED).
- Glasses integration — "coming soon" with no date is a non-event; cut
  rather than spend a line on a promise with no shape yet.
- A full list of every competing agent (Copilot Cowork, Nova Act detail,
  etc.) — three names (Google, OpenAI, Amazon) carry "you're late,
  not first" without turning into a list the viewer has to track.

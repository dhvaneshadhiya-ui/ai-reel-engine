# Structure — mac-multiple-headphones

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`frameworks/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A Mac user who wants to share a movie with someone on two pairs of headphones
discovers the Mac only plays to one pair, that Apple's own workaround costs
them their volume keys, and that a free app does it in one toggle — which
matters because it turns a buried audio-engineer setting into a two-second
thing, with one honest caveat about sync.

## SHAPE (S17)

Tutorial with a problem → workaround → better tool → caveat arc. It fits
because the viewer's question is practical ("can I do this?"), there is a real
built-in answer that is worse, and the recommended app has a real limitation
the viewer needs before they rely on it.

## PROMISE (S2)

One Mac playing to two pairs of headphones at once — and the easiest way to do it.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: "There is a built-in fix." — followed immediately by "The catch", so the
viewer waits for the better way.
Paid off: "PairPods does the same job with one toggle", and the ending returns to
the promise with the one condition that decides whether it works well
("matching pairs work best").

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: macOS can output to several devices through a Multi-Output Device, and
PairPods wraps that in a menu-bar toggle.
WHY: the built-in route disables keyboard/menu-bar volume and is buried in Audio
MIDI Setup.
SO WHAT: two people can watch one Mac with their own volume, for free — as long
as their headphones play nicely together.

# Structure — apple-pencil-iphone-ultra

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`styles/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).
`script_approval.py propose` refuses while placeholders remain.

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

One sentence, before anything else:
A viewer who assumes Apple is finally bringing an Apple Pencil to iPhone —
because Apple already sells one for iPad — discovers instead that Apple
built one, tested it on the foldable iPhone Ultra, and buried it, because
the phone Apple is about to call its most premium iPhone ever apparently
can't survive a pointed tip touching its own screen.

## SHAPE (S17)

News.

Chosen because there is exactly one hard reported fact today (Apple tested
a stylus and shelved it, per Gurman) sitting inside a reveal about WHY —
and the why is what actually matters, not the accessory. A Discovery shape
would work too, but News keeps the confirmed-vs-rumored line explicit,
which this story needs: the event date is official, the foldable's very
existence is still "expected," and the pencil story itself is single-source
Gurman reporting repeated by everyone else, not independently confirmed.

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you'll know exactly what Apple built, exactly why it got killed,
and what that decision quietly admits about how fragile Apple's first
foldable screen really is — days before Apple has to convince you it's
durable enough to actually use.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook states Apple tested a stylus for its own foldable phone
and then hid it — "Apple built a Pencil for its folding iPhone. Then it
made sure you'd never see it." — without yet saying why.
Paid off: the reveal beat gives the real reason (the tip risks marking the
internal display, not just "awkward to hold"), and the closer returns to
the planted tension by naming what it means for next week: the same
company that shows off a screen built to fold thousands of times won't let
its own accessory touch it.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: Apple prototyped and tested a stubbier, magnetic Apple Pencil sized
for the foldable iPhone Ultra, then decided not to ship it.
WHY: parking it on the phone's edge made the phone awkward to hold and to
open, and — the bigger problem — the pointed tip risked leaving marks on
the delicate internal foldable glass.
SO WHAT: that second reason is a tell. Apple is about to pitch this as its
most premium iPhone yet, and its own internal test found the screen can't
take contact from a pen tip — a durability admission dressed up as an
accessory that quietly didn't make the cut.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- "iPhone Ultra" as a confirmed product name — it's still leak/rumor
  naming, not an Apple-confirmed title, so the script hedges it rather
  than treating it as settled the way the Sept 9 date is.
- Full 2007 keynote context (crowd reaction, the rest of that riff on
  keyboards/styluses) — kept to one callback line; more would turn a
  6-second beat into a history lesson.
- Any comparison to Samsung/S Pen or other stylus-equipped foldables —
  none of the sourced reporting makes that comparison, and adding one
  would be an invented claim, not a reported one.
- Charging wattage, pencil price, exact dimensions — not in either source;
  inventing specifics here is exactly the failure mode research.md exists
  to block.
- Any implication that a leaked photo or render of the actual prototype
  exists — it doesn't. Visuals stay confined to what's real: the coverage
  itself, the existing iPad Apple Pencil for comparison, and the official
  Sept 9 invite/keynote material already verified for apple-surprise-and-shine.

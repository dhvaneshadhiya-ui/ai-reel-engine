# Structure — whatsapp-agents

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who believes every WhatsApp chat is end-to-end encrypted discovers
that WhatsApp is building a chat that, by WhatsApp's own definition, is not —
which matters because the same company spent the last year throwing outside
AI assistants off the platform, and this is the door it is building back.

## SHAPE (S17)

**Discovery, told as a reversal.** Not "Product announcement" — nothing has
been announced, and treating a beta teardown as a launch would be the wrong
certainty. Discovery fits what actually happened (someone found it in an APK)
and it earns the reversal ending, because the interesting part is not the
feature, it is what the feature contradicts.

## PROMISE (S2)

You will learn what this new kind of chat can and cannot see, and why the
company that banned outside AI is now inviting it back in.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: sentence 3 — "Which is a strange thing for the encryption app to
build. Until you look at what it threw out first."
Paid off: the timeline beat — October 2025's ban, in force by 15 January 2026,
with the EU, Italy and Brazil opening antitrust probes over it. The ending
returns to the loop without resolving it dishonestly: nobody has said the two
are connected, and the reel says exactly that (S18).

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: up to five third-party agents, each with a name and photo you choose,
each in its own chat.
WHY: they run through a Meta service on the developer's behalf, so the
developer can read what you send — that is why WhatsApp does not call them
end-to-end encrypted.
SO WHAT: those chats sit in the same list as your real ones and look the same,
because you are the one who names them. The thing to carry away is not fear —
personal chats are untouched, and the agent is fenced to its own conversation —
it is knowing which chat is which.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- **Meta Business Agent, launched globally 3 June 2026.** A clean third beat
  for the timeline and genuinely relevant. Cut because the reel already asks
  the viewer to hold two dates, and a third turns a story into a chronology.
- **The California class action** alleging Meta staff could read messages. Cut
  on evidence: it is contested, Meta calls it "categorically false and absurd",
  and welding a disputed allegation to a sourced beta finding would make the
  reel say more than it can prove.
- **Build number 2.26.35.3.** True, sourced, and means nothing to a viewer.
- **"Agents cannot be added to groups or communities."** A real limit, but it
  competes with the stronger scope line about contacts and media.
- **The accuracy disclaimer** ("responses may be inaccurate"). Every AI ships
  one; it is not news.

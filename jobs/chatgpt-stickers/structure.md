# Structure — chatgpt-stickers

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`styles/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who believes custom sticker packs need a cutout app, a paid tool and
a lot of fiddling discovers that ChatGPT now does the whole job from one
photo — which matters because the thing standing between them and a pack of
their own face in WhatsApp is about four taps, not a skill they lack.

## SHAPE (S17)

**Tutorial**, cold-opened on its own payoff.

It fits because the value here is entirely procedural: the feature is real,
free and already installed, so nothing needs arguing — only showing. The one
risk of a pure tutorial is a flat opening ("here's a new ChatGPT feature"),
so the reel opens on the finished nine-sticker grid and works backwards into
the how. The `ai-tools` skeleton's face bookends carry the open and the CTA;
the middle belongs to the recordings.

## PROMISE (S2)

You will see exactly how to turn one photo into a nine-sticker WhatsApp pack,
start to finish, in the app already on your phone — including the one rule
that stops most people's first export.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: beat 1, "That's my own face, and ChatGPT built the whole pack." The
grid is on screen before any explanation exists, so the question the viewer
carries is *how did that get made, and can I do it.*

Paid off: beat 11, "Name the pack, and it's sitting in your keyboard." The
reel returns to the same artifact it opened on, now arriving where the viewer
would actually use it. The loop closes on the destination, not on a summary.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

- **WHAT:** ChatGPT ships native sticker generation with transparent
  backgrounds and one-tap export to WhatsApp and iMessage.
- **WHY:** the background cutout — the step that used to require a separate
  app — now happens inside the generation, and the export writes a real
  WhatsApp pack rather than nine loose images.
- **SO WHAT:** the whole pipeline that used to be "generate, download, cut
  out, import to a sticker app, export" collapses into four taps, free, on a
  phone. That is the viewer's actual gain, and it is why the reel spends its
  middle on the taps rather than on the announcement.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- **The golden-retriever text-only prompt** (from the user's first draft). We
  have no footage of a text-only pack, and Rule 3 forbids saying it over
  footage of something else. The template beat replaces it and is a better
  fact.
- **"Style Lock — bold outline, white border."** Not a feature; no source
  anywhere and nothing in the app calls it that. Cut outright.
- **"18 aesthetics."** Wrong. Corrected to nineteen, counted in the app.
- **The 30-sticker WhatsApp maximum.** True and sourced, but the pack maxes
  at nine anyway, so the ceiling never binds the viewer. Density without
  momentum.
- **Images 2.0 as the underlying model, and the 24 Aug launch date.** Both
  sourced, neither changes what the viewer does next.
- **iMessage as a second export target.** Real, and on screen in the export
  menu, but naming two destinations splits a single clean instruction. The
  menu is visible; the narration commits to WhatsApp.
- **The "Think harder" toggle** visible in the recording. Irrelevant to
  stickers, and naming it would invite a question the reel does not answer.

## SOURCES

Full ledger in `research.md`. Two independent origins throughout:

1. **OpenAI's own announcement** (24 Aug 2026) — the feature, transparent
   backgrounds, free/mobile/global.
2. **Hands-on testing** — 9to5Mac's, and our own three screen recordings
   (2026-09-01), which are the primary evidence for the entry path, the
   template shape, the nineteen styles and every export step.
3. **WhatsApp's own developer documentation** — primary source for the
   3-minimum / 30-maximum pack rule.

Recorded disagreement: outlets that name a style count say 18; the app shows
19. Own testing wins, and the reel says nineteen.

# Structure — iphone18-colors

Written BEFORE the first sentence, per writing-block-2.0 §17.
Framework copy: `references/writing-block-2.0.md`.

## SHAPE

**Discovery**, not News and not a List.

- *News* has no engine: nothing happened. Apple has announced nothing.
- *List* is what the source is — a guide enumerating colors — and §3 says a
  ten-fact article must not become ten sentences.

Discovery fits what the material actually contains: Hook → Context → Mystery →
Investigation → Reveal → Implication. There is a real mystery (nobody agrees
what the color looks like), a real investigation (it has a published Pantone
code), a real reveal (Pantone files it under Red-Purple — it's a plum), and a
real implication (which phone gets the bold color tells you who Apple thinks is
buying it).

## OPENING = HOOK + CONTEXT + PROMISE (§16)

The previous draft opened "Apple's new iPhone red isn't red." That is the §16
failure verbatim — a paradox with no premise. A cold viewer does not know which
iPhone, which red, or why they should care.

Opening now establishes the premise in sentence one ("Apple's next iPhone has a
new signature color"), the mystery in sentence two, and the promise in
sentences three and four.

## PROMISE (§2)

Two things the viewer is told they will get:
1. What the color actually looks like — settled, not guessed.
2. Why Apple's most expensive iPhone reportedly doesn't get it.

## OPEN LOOP (§10)

**Planted:** sentence 4 — "Then the strange part — Apple's priciest iPhone
doesn't get it."
**Paid off:** ~55s, "Now — the expensive one," through to the volume/risk
conclusion.
**Returned to at the end (§18):** the CTA re-asks the opening question with the
answer now in hand — "plum, not red. Still the one you'd pick?"

## WHAT → WHY → SO WHAT (§7)

The Pantone code is the WHAT. It is worthless to a viewer on its own, and the
last draft stopped there. The SO WHAT is now explicit: *"if you're picturing a
rich red iPhone, the real one is quieter and browner."* That is the sentence
that converts a print-industry number into something the viewer can act on.

## ESCALATION (§6)

expectation → reality (renders vs Pantone), then small detail → major
implication (a color range → what Apple believes about who buys each phone).

## WHAT WAS CUT (§11, §21)

- **Cosmic Orange being replaced.** True and tempting, but it is an iPhone 17
  color and explains nothing the story needs.
- **The "uniform back" section.** It is about how color is applied, not which
  colors — a different subject arriving mid-investigation.
- **Pantone codes for the other three (427, 426, 2121).** Numbers for their own
  sake. 6076 earns its place because the whole investigation turns on it; the
  other three would be density, not momentum.

# Structure — apple-trade-in-value-in

Written BEFORE the first sentence. Framework:
`styles/shortform-script-framework.md` (S17 shapes; S25 standard).

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who thinks of trade-in as one number for their phone discovers that
Apple publishes the whole depreciation curve on the same page it advertises the
top figure, and that a flagship loses about three quarters of its ceiling in
five generations, which matters because the cost of waiting is roughly Rs 8,000
a year whether the phone is used or not.

## SHAPE (S17)

**Explainer**, format `news`. Deliberately NOT the tutorial shape used by the
USD cut. That reel answers "how do I find my number"; this one answers "what is
holding on actually costing me". Same source page, different question, and
they must not be the same video: the first version of this job WAS the how-to
with the currency swapped, which is one reel published twice.

Format changed from `howto` to `news` for that reason. There are no steps to
follow here, so the howto profile's long screen holds would be wrong - this
cut is card-paced.

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you know what Apple currently pays for six generations of the same
flagship, which single year costs the most, and roughly what a year of holding
on is worth.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: "This isn't a bad offer, it's a curve" - the viewer is told a shape
exists before being shown it.
Paid off: at the ladder, and closed at "roughly eight thousand rupees a year",
which converts the shape into the viewer's own cost (S18).

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: 57,000 / 48,000 / 39,500 / 32,000 / 21,500 / 15,500 across six Pro Max
generations.
WHY: every figure is a ceiling, and the steepest single step is not the newest
one but 13 to 12 Pro Max.
SO WHAT: waiting has a price, about Rs 8,300 a generation, and the real offer
sits below every number shown.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Launch prices. They would sharpen the story enormously and are exactly what
  the ledger cannot support, so they are absent rather than hedged.
- The purchase-tied "Shop iPhone" framing and the Android line - both belong
  to the how-to cut, and one reel, one idea.
- The non-Pro-Max models. 32 rows is a table, not a story; one tier makes the
  curve legible.
- The US comparison (US retains 22%, India 27%). Interesting, but it splits
  the reel's attention across two markets.

# Structure — five-free-ai-tools

## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who believes "free AI tools" means crippled trials discovers that five of
them are genuinely free with published ceilings, which matters because the ceiling —
not the price — is what decides whether a tool fits their week.

## SHAPE (S17)

**List**, with a spine. A bare list ("five free tools") is a queue of names and is
forgettable by slide three. The spine here is the CEILING: every item answers the same
question — where does free stop? — so the five entries accumulate into one idea instead
of five unrelated recommendations. It also keeps the reel honest: the interesting number
is always the limit, never the word "free".

## PROMISE (S2)

By the end you know, for five tools, exactly how much you get before you are asked to pay.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook — "the question is where free stops" — which promises a ceiling for each
one rather than a recommendation.
Paid off: the close returns to it and turns it: the ceilings themselves move, and Copilot's
moved more than once this year. The loop closes on the same idea it opened, one level up
(S18) — which is also the reason to check before planning around any of them.

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: five tools with real free tiers and published limits.
WHY: vendors now publish the ceiling instead of hiding it behind a trial, because compute
costs money and they would rather cap you than bill you by surprise.
SO WHAT: you can plan around a number. Fifty chats a day is a working day; ten thousand
credits a month is a few voiceovers, not a series; no commercial rights means a hobby, not
a client job.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- The Gemini API free-tier tightening (2.5 Pro removed, Flash requests cut). True and
  relevant to developers, but it is a different product from AI Studio and would need its
  own sentence to avoid implying AI Studio got worse.
- Copilot Free's chat-request number. GitHub's own pages state completions but not chat, and
  an unsourced figure on screen is exactly what G68 refuses.
- Perplexity and DeepSeek, both strong candidates. Perplexity's help centre blocks fetching
  and DeepSeek's site does not state its free-chat limits, so neither could be verified to
  the standard the other five met. Cut rather than hedged.
- A ranking. "Best" would need a test we have not run; the reel states ceilings instead.

## SOURCES

Vendor pages for every ceiling (ai.google.dev, support.google.com/gemininotebook,
elevenlabs.io, suno.com, docs.github.com), plus independent reporting for the two claims
that are about CHANGE rather than price: Google's own announcement and Android Police for
the five-hour reset, heise and Roboin for Copilot's 2026 changes. Full list with VIA lines
in research.md.

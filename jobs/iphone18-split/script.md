Apple just skipped the iPhone 18.
For the first time in 7 years, there's no standard iPhone this September.
Pegatron — one of the companies that actually builds iPhones — just confirmed it on this week's earnings call.
Apple is splitting the iPhone 18 launch in two.
Wave 1, September 2026: the iPhone 18 Pro, the 18 Pro Max — and reportedly Apple's first foldable, the iPhone Ultra.
Wave 2, March 2027: the standard iPhone 18, the budget 18e, and the iPhone Air 2.
Every number iPhone from the iPhone 11 to the iPhone 17 launched in September. 7 generations straight.
So why break it now? Simply put, September becomes a pure premium show.
Pro models carry Apple's fattest margins, and they get the entire holiday quarter to themselves.
On top of it, Pegatron says component shortages — chips and memory — could run into 2028.
And look, if parts are scarce, you don't want 6 new iPhones fighting for them at once.
Anyways, this has been building for a year — analyst Ming-Chi Kuo called a spring 2027 iPhone 18 months ago.
It's just the first time the supply chain said it out loud.
To be fair, Apple hasn't confirmed a thing. And in leaks like this, dates are always the softest detail.
If it sticks, the iPhone becomes a twice-a-year event.
So — wait till March, or is Apple betting you'll just buy the Pro?

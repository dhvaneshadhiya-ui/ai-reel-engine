## STORY ENGINE (framework §4A)

A viewer who just watched Apple's September 9 keynote assumes that was the
year's Apple news, discovers Apple already has seven more products lined up
for October — and then discovers the one everyone's actually been waiting
years for, the touchscreen MacBook, is the one most likely to miss the wave
entirely, which matters because it means the hype and the calendar have
quietly come apart.

## SHAPE (S17)

**News**, with a light **List** structure underneath it (the products are
the spine) — same combination as `apple-8-products-september`, but a
DIFFERENT treatment of it: that reel sorted its eight into two static
buckets (confirmed vs. rumored) up front and stayed in that frame throughout.
This one runs the list in one motion, category by category (Mac -> TV ->
iPad -> Home), building toward a single twist at the end rather than
declaring the split at the top — the honesty beat IS the ending, not a
label attached to half the list. Chosen because this story has one specific
reveal (the touchscreen Mac's slip) worth holding back, where the September
reel had a symmetric split with no single item worth holding back.

## PROMISE (S2)

You'll know exactly what's landing in Apple's second fall wave this October
— and which of it, despite the headlines, might not actually make it.

## OPEN LOOP (S10)

Planted: the hook's claim that the September keynote "wasn't the finale" —
implies something specific is still coming that the viewer doesn't know
about yet, without naming it.
Paid off: the closing turn names it directly — the touchscreen MacBook,
the most-hyped item in the whole wave, is reportedly the one that slips to
2027 — converting the hook's vague tension into a concrete, specific
reversal, then hands the viewer the takeaway (temper expectations on the
headline item, expect the smaller refreshes on schedule).

## WHAT -> WHY -> SO WHAT (S7)

WHAT: Apple has 7+ more products moving in October across Mac (M6 MacBook
Pro, M6 iMac), TV (new Apple TV 4K), iPad (first OLED iPad mini) and Home
(touchscreen home hub, HomePod mini 2, HomePod 3) — on top of iPhone Duo,
which Apple already confirmed ships October 23.
WHY: the smart-home products have reportedly been sitting "very nearly
ready" and were held back specifically until the new AI Siri (officially
announced at WWDC, now in iOS 27 Release Candidate) is ready to power them
— which is also why the timing lines up the way it does. The touchscreen
MacBook was supposed to be part of the same wave, but a memory-chip
shortage — driven by AI server demand eating the global RAM supply — has
reportedly pushed it out.
SO WHAT: a viewer excited for "seven new Apple products in October" should
expect the smaller, more corroborated refreshes to land on schedule, and
should not expect the one product most people actually want — the
touchscreen Mac — this month, despite it being reported as part of the same
wave as recently as a few months ago.

## WHAT WAS CUT (S11, S21)

- Full chip specs for M6 (core counts, node process) — a spec dump kills
  momentum in a 7-item list; the chip name plus "which products get it" is
  what a viewer deciding whether to care needs.
- iPad mini exact chip uncertainty (A19 Pro vs. A20 Pro — sources disagree
  on which) — the OLED screen and the water resistance are the headline
  upgrades; the chip argument is a rabbit hole for one beat in a fast list.
- HomePod 3 and HomePod mini 2 treated as one "smart home reset" line
  instead of two separate beats — they share one cause (waiting on AI
  Siri) and separating them would cost two more sentences to say the same
  thing twice.
- The base iPad (A18) — multiple sources say it may slip to early 2027, so
  it doesn't clearly belong in an "October" list at all; including it would
  have undercut the same honesty the reel spends its ending on.
- Mac Studio, also reportedly delayed by the same memory shortage as the
  touchscreen MacBook — not part of Apple's own confirmed or strongly-multi
  October wave, and mentioning a second delayed product would blunt the
  twist ending by making the "one that might not show up" no longer feel
  singular.
- Vision Pro — not part of this wave; last updated with M5 in a separate
  cycle, mixing it in would break the "one wave" framing.

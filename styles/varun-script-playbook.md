## The skeleton (his default 6 acts)

**Our budget is 160-215 words → 60-80s at speed 1.05 (gate G02), not his
native 120-260 → 40-60s.** (2026-08-13: re-measured — this voice delivers
~2.7 wps, not the 3.2 the old 220-300 figure assumed; three masters agree.) Same six acts, more room in PROOF STACK and
MECHANISM — never padding, and never a sentence that fails the test 'does
this change what the viewer understands?'

1. **HOOK** (1–2 sentences, cold)
2. **NAME IT** — "It's called X." / "Meet X." (only after the hook lands)
3. **PROOF STACK** — 2–4 rapid concrete demos/specs, each ONE sentence, each
   with a number. This is where footage lives.
4. **MECHANISM** — "That's because… / Here's why that's a big deal… / See,
   normally…" — one clean explainer, analogy welcome.
5. **ZOOM OUT** — "Anyways," + the bigger meaning / spec dump / industry shift
   ("the era of X being behind is officially over", "imagine what phones from
   2030 will do"). Often includes the HONESTY BEAT and/or PERSONAL STAKE.
6. **CTA** — one of the four types below. Last line, short.

## Hook taxonomy (with his actual openers — match one, never invent a 6th)

1. **"X just did Y" news-shock** — subject + just + verb + concrete payload:
   - "China just dropped Kimi K3 and it's as fast as Fable 5 at UI design."
   - "This engineer just discovered a hack that gives us 12% faster CPUs by
     just adding the number 3 to a file."
   - "Someone just built Tom Riddle's diary from Harry Potter using Claude."
2. **Superlative claim** — "the most / number one / first ever" + proof promise:
   - "There's finally a free open-source image generator that just became
     number one overall in the world."
   - "This hand by 1X is one of the most advanced robotic hands you'll ever see."
3. **Absurd image / story cold-open** — a sentence that shouldn't be true:
   - "Tung tung tung sahur is in an American federal court right now. And a
     judge is about to decide who actually owns him."
   - "Having a baby in the future could literally start with a sample of your blood."
4. **Contrarian / myth-bust** — break the assumed frame in line one:
   - "Apple basically gave up trying to fix Siri on their own, so they called Google."
   - "Most founders say they know their target customer… That's not a customer.
     That's a category."
5. **Analogy hook** — absurd relatable comparison, then map it:
   - "Have you ever tried describing a tattoo over the phone? Hopefully no.
     However, that's exactly what working with agentic loops feels like."

Rules: concrete noun + strong verb + a number or superlative. Zero throat-
clearing. Frequently followed by "Here's how that's even possible." or a
visual point: "Just look at these yourself." (see Visual deixis).

## Rhythm + language rules (measured)

- Short declaratives, ~8–14 words, ONE idea each. Then occasionally one long
  spec sentence deliberately PACKED with numbers (the "spec dump").
- **Number density**: a number every 2–3 sentences ($3.24, 25 degrees of
  freedom, sub-0.2mm, 12.7 million players, 499 units, 2.8T params). A claim
  without a number is a claim he cuts.
- Connective glossary (use these, not corporate transitions):
  "That's because…", "Here's why that's a big deal.", "See, normally…",
  "Meaning,", "Simply put,", "On top of it,", "Not to mention", "What's really
  clever is…", "But the real story is what's underneath.", "Now the important
  bit is…", "But here's why this time feels different."
- **"Anyways,"** = his signature act-break into zoom-out/spec dump. Use once.
- Rhetorical questions sparingly (≤2), mostly at hook or CTA.
- Casual register: "kind of cool", "pretty insane", "to be frank", "look,".
  Contractions always. No em-dash-speak, no "delve", no listicle voice.

## The honesty beat (almost every reel has ONE)

A one-to-two sentence deflation that buys credibility right before or inside
the zoom-out: "But we're really early… a mouse is not a human." / "Apple has
made these exact promises before, and we all know how that went, so I'm taking
it with a pinch of salt." / "While it still ranks lower on some benchmarks…"
NEVER skip this on hype topics.

## Personal stake (~half the reels)

First-person, brief: "And to be frank, this is my favorite thing about AI right
now." / "I'll keep testing it internally and keep you guys posted." the creator's
version can carry his creator/builder identity ("I make videos for a living…").

## CTA menu (pick exactly one)

1. **Comment-keyword**: "Comment the word FREE and I'll send you the link."
2. **Question CTA**: "So what do you think — are you going to buy it?" /
   "Does Triple T belong to a company, or to the internet?"
3. **Build-prompt**: "Tell me in the comments what you're planning to build with it."
4. **Follow-up promise**: "I'll keep testing and keep you posted."

## Visual deixis (the manifest hook — CRITICAL for our pipeline)

He writes lines that POINT at the footage: "Just look at these yourself." /
"just look at this thing" / "Just watch this." Use these ONLY when the manifest
has a spectacular clip bound to that beat — the line is a contract that the
next 2–3 seconds are pure footage (no MG, captions low).

## Binding rules for the Script Director

- Every PROOF-STACK sentence must bind to a manifest asset that LITERALLY shows
  it. No asset → the sentence doesn't exist. Rewrite around what we have.
- MECHANISM beats bind to MG (SpecSheet, diagrams, HCompare) — numbers must
  carry units on screen.
- HOOK must bind to the single most spectacular clip in the manifest (sound-off
  test: can a viewer name the subject from the hook frame alone?).
- HONESTY BEAT + PERSONAL STAKE + CTA = facecam beats (no asset needed).
- Target 160-215 words for the 60-80s band at the measured 2.7 wps; hard cap 216 (2026-08-13, supersedes the 3.2-wps figures).

## Anti-patterns (things he never does — reject drafts containing them)

- Throat-clearing openers ("In today's video", "Let's talk about", "So recently")
- Unnumbered hype ("insanely powerful", "game-changing" without a stat)
- More than one CTA; CTAs longer than one sentence
- Explaining the joke / repeating the hook at the end
- Passive voice in the hook; "we" when he means "I"
- Claims with no receipt and no footage (cut or move to facecam opinion)
